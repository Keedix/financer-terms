Privacy policy

Financer · last updated 3 November 2026

Financer is a personal finance application run by {{NAME}} ("I") for private, non-commercial use, on a server I own. It is not offered to the public. This page says what data it handles when I connect my own bank accounts through Enable Banking.

Who is responsible

{{NAME}}, contact: {{EMAIL}}. The application is used by me and, if I give them an account, by people in my household, each seeing only their own data. It is used for purely personal and household purposes.

What data is handled

  • Account information from the bank, only for the accounts I choose to connect: account names, IBANs, balances, and transactions (date, amount, description, the other party's name and account number).
  • What I enter myself: accounts, categories, rules, recurring payments and notes, and transactions I import from bank files.
  • Technical data to run the connection: an identifier of the bank session and when my consent ends. I do not store my bank login details; I log in at the bank itself.

Why and on what basis

To see my balances and spending in one place. The data is read only after I give my explicit consent at the bank, and only for as long as that consent lasts.

Where it is stored and who sees it

  • The data is stored in a database on my own server and in my own backups of it. It is not sent to any other service, analytics or advertising provider.
  • The application can only be reached from my private network.
  • Bank data is fetched through Enable Banking, a licensed account information service provider, which handles it under its own privacy policy.
  • I do not sell, share or publish the data.

How long

As long as I use the application. The connection to a bank ends when the consent expires (the bank sets the period) or when I remove it, and I can then delete the imported data. Removing a connection also ends the session at Enable Banking.

Your rights

If you are a person whose data I process, you may ask me for access to it, correction or deletion, and you may complain to your data protection authority (in Poland, the President of the Personal Data Protection Office, UODO). Write to {{EMAIL}}.

Changes

If this changes, the date above changes with it.
